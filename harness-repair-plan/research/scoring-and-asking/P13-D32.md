## P13-1: the BA panel and its QA review

**Where the panel runs.**

| Command | BAs |
|---|---|
| `/spec` | three, in parallel; the spec agent writes the spec from their analyses and the owner's answers, each decision recorded in the spec |
| `/dev`, `/redev`, `/dev-command` with a spec | one: it checks that the spec still matches the code, splits it into dev tasks, and asks only what the spec left open (at least one round, D29) |
| the same without a spec | three, in parallel |

**The three BAs.** Independent: none sees another's output. Each takes one angle:
- A: root cause first, traced to the code, with evidence (file and line, log or trace);
- B: the smallest change that meets the requirement;
- C: the owner's intent and the product view.

Each writes its own report: the root cause with its evidence, at least two solutions with their trade-offs, its
recommendation, and the points that need the owner. Which model each BA runs on is decided by experiment, on cost
against how often its analysis decides the outcome (D18).

**The flow.**
1. The three BAs run in parallel.
2. **Panel QA**, one QA dispatch, reads the three reports. It checks each cited piece of evidence and marks every root
   cause as verified, unsupported or refuted. It lists where the BAs agree, and turns each disagreement into a
   decision point with its options, recommended first. An unsupported or refuted root cause does not become an option.
3. The orchestrator asks the owner the decision points (AskUserQuestion, no round cap, D29). D18's line: the owner
   decides the solution level (what to build, how far, the trade-off between scope, risk and speed); anything a
   measurement can answer is decided by experiment.
4. **The final BA context** has one producer (D24): the BA whose option the owner chose, re-dispatched with the
   answers. When the BAs agreed, it is the BA whose evidence panel QA verified best.
5. The existing BA-QA check validates that context, then dev starts.

## P13-2: score events and /rating

New events, written by hooks from facts like the others:
- `ba_option_chosen`: the owner chose this BA's option (a gain);
- `ba_root_cause_refuted`: panel QA marked its root cause refuted, or a later QA showed it wrong (a loss).

`/rating` (a new command, terminal only, no HTML): for each agent, the score and rank, a ▁▂▃▅▇ trend over its last
10 cycles, its last 5 events with task id and reason, and the owner's last ratings.

## P13-3: praise, and what a low score costs

**Praise, not threats.** The dispatch view names the agent's recent good events as facts, for example: "your last
cycle passed QA on the first round and the owner rated it 5 stars; keep that standard". The sentence promising
"punishment far exceeding any other event, irreversible" goes.

**Low score** (the two lowest ranks to start with; the threshold is then set by experiment, D18):
- dev: after its normal QA, one more QA round by an independent QA dispatch;
- BA: after its normal BA-QA check, one more QA round by an independent QA dispatch;
- QA: a Codex debate (under D31's protocol) on every one of its rounds, even without `--codex`.

When the score climbs back above the threshold, the extra round stops.

## Acceptance additions

- A replay of `/dev` without a spec where two BAs disagree on the root cause: panel QA turns the disagreement into one
  decision point, the owner's answer picks the producer of the final context, and the score log gains
  `ba_option_chosen` for that BA.
- A replay where panel QA refutes one BA's root cause: that option never reaches the owner, and the log gains
  `ba_root_cause_refuted`.
- A replay with a low-score dev: two QA dispatches judge its work, the second independent of the first; with the score
  restored, one.
- A replay with a low-score QA and no `--codex`: its round includes a Codex debate.
- `/rating` on a fixture log prints the score, rank, trend and last events for each agent.
